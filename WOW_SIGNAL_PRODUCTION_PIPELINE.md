## 📡 THE WOW! SIGNAL — The Call We Never Answered — Full Production Pipeline (90s TikTok)

**Topic:** On the night of August 15, 1977, a radio telescope in Ohio — Ohio State's "Big Ear" — recorded something that has never been explained. For seventy-two seconds, a strong, narrow signal poured down out of the dark from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius. It was tuned almost exactly to 1420 MHz — the hydrogen line, the one frequency scientists had argued a distant civilization would most likely choose to broadcast on, because it is universal across the cosmos. The reading climbed and fell in the exact shape you'd expect from a fixed point in the sky drifting through the telescope's view. An astronomer named Jerry Ehman, going through the printout days later, circled the sequence "6EQUJ5" in red ink and wrote a single word in the margin: *Wow*. It was, by the textbook, exactly what a message from someone else should look like. And then it was gone. We aimed every instrument we had back at that patch of sky. We listened for hours, for years, for decades. It never came again — not once. Maybe it was interference, a satellite, a flare, a trick of our own machines. Or maybe, for seventy-two seconds, we were not alone — and we missed our only chance to answer. We will never know which is true. The part of you that hopes someone is out there is the same part that keeps listening.
**Duration:** 90 seconds → 18 scenes × 5 sec
**Format:** Vertical 9:16, cinematic anamorphic, 1080×1920 (TikTok native)
**Voice:** Female contralto, intimate documentary, ElevenLabs v3 (dynamic flow — no static pauses)
**Visual reference:** Denis Villeneuve "Arrival" awe × Robert Zemeckis "Contact" observatory longing × Christopher Nolan "Interstellar" deep-space loneliness × Stanley Kubrick "2001" cosmic dread. Lonely, cosmic, reverent, intimate — the silence after a sound that never returns.
